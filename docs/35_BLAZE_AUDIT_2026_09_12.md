## Changes audited

- `run_table_loop` swaps the accepted endpoint snapshot and carries its
  acceleration into the next step, reducing duplicate endpoint work;
- `propagate_sampled_extend` validates arrays, finite state/time, and step
  consistency, and rolls back lengths after a partial failure;
- SIMD wrappers check `base.checked_add(width)` before pointer arithmetic;
- scalar gravity skips non-gravitating bodies before distance singularity
  checks;
- `trim_before` removes an accumulated stale block instead of repeated moves;
- zero budgets are respected by head baking and scaled display paths;
- coast batching avoids speculative allocator work and keeps assisted rotation
  on the stepped path;
- `rails_time_advanced_s` is included in effective advancement, overlay, and
  CSV capture, with old JSON defaulting it to zero.

## Historical measurement

The original command was:

```bash
cargo bench -p thessa-sim-core --bench onrails
```

For a 22-source system and 40,000 steps/40,001 points, the recorded baseline
was `24.011 ms` and the post-change median was `18.870 ms`, with seven repeats
between `18.609` and `20.465 ms`. Warm-cache samples were `4.7–5.2 µs` at p50.

This measured CPU baking, not game FPS. The baseline had one run, so the
relative gain is indicative rather than a release guarantee.

## Boundaries

Batch coast is allowed only for zero aero load and certified absence of contact
over the interval. A mode named `Coast` does not itself mean vacuum; the
configured atmosphere can return non-zero density at altitude. Asymmetric
free rotation is not replaced by a constant angular-velocity shortcut.

Trimming releases old samples but does not reset the accepted-step horizon; a
new bake is still required when the total horizon is exhausted. This audit did
not prove infinite extension of one trajectory, nor did it include a separate
visual/FPS run.
